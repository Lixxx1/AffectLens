# Tachisme

## Definition And Historical Context

Tachisme is the direction within Art Informel that most strongly emphasizes "stains, spots, drips, and spontaneous traces." Its name comes from the French `tache`, meaning spot or stain. Tate treats Tachisme as a mode of abstract painting that often overlaps with Art Informel; Britannica also places Tachism in the context of postwar Parisian abstraction, stressing its similarities to American action painting while identifying it as part of the European development of non-geometric abstraction. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/t/tachisme); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Tachism)

The point of Tachisme is not to make an image into "pretty stains," but to let accidental diffusion, dripping, and splashing of paint become visible evidence of psychological state and creative process. It often carries the instability, fracture, and anti-order feeling of postwar European art. [Tate Art informel](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/art-informel); [Britannica Art Informel](https://www.britannica.com/art/Art-Informel)

## Visual Identification

- **Stains and spots**: Paint appears as patches, drips, splashes, bleeding, rubbed marks, or irregular color masses.
- **Rapid gesture**: Sweeps, splashes, and broken lines show bodily movement and immediate reaction.
- **Non-geometric space**: The image avoids stable grids or clear geometric structure, looking more like accidental sedimentation.
- **Unstable color**: Dark colors, muddy colors, gray, red-black conflict, or abrupt bright accents often intensify anxiety.
- **Blank space and density**: Empty areas can provide breathing room, while dense stains increase visual pressure.

## Emotion And Valence/Arousal

Tachisme usually has high arousal because stains, splashes, and rapid gestures make the viewing experience feel close to release, anxiety, chaos, ecstasy, or loss of control. Valence depends on color and density: bright color blocks and open blank space can make the emotion feel free and active; dark colors, dirty colors, crowded stains, and scraping push valence negative or mixed.

In output, you might write: "The splashed black lines and irregular red patches raise arousal; the gray-brown ground and compressed space lower valence; local blank space prevents the emotion from becoming fully suffocating, so the overall effect is mixed valence with high arousal."

## Common Misreadings

- Do not classify every splash or stain as Tachisme; decorative abstraction can imitate this surface.
- When distinguishing it from American action painting, consider artist, date, postwar European context, and material texture.
- When distinguishing it from Art Brut, remember that Tachisme belongs to a professional abstract-art context and is not defined by the maker's social position.
