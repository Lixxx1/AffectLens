# Dubuffet / Fautrier Adjacent Directions

## Definition And Historical Context

Dubuffet / Fautrier adjacent directions refer to areas of Art Informel associated with Jean Dubuffet and Jean Fautrier: rough surfaces, material experimentation, bodily suggestion, anti-cultural stance, and postwar trauma. Centre Pompidou's materials on Fautrier often place him in the context of wartime and postwar materiality, bodily suggestion, and the dissolution of form; Dubuffet also connects to Art Brut collecting, anti-academic aesthetics, and rough material experiments. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/personne/cK4bBX); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/1633)

This direction is not necessarily pure abstraction: works may suggest bodies, faces, skin, flesh, walls, scars, or ruins, but these images are often swallowed by heavy material and rough surfaces. It is heavier than Lyrical Abstraction and carries more historical and bodily pressure than decorative textured painting. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/art-informel); [Collection de l’Art Brut](https://artbrut.ch/en/history)

## Visual Identification

- **Bodily suggestion**: Faces, torsos, skin, flesh masses, wounds, outlines, or body fragments appear indistinctly.
- **Rough surface**: Stucco, thick paste, scraping, cracks, sand, stone, and dirty colors create a wall-like or wound-like feeling.
- **Anti-academic figures**: Figures may be distorted, flat, roughly outlined, childlike, or close to Art Brut visual language.
- **Postwar weight**: Low value, damaged surfaces, and uncomfortable materials evoke ruins or trauma.
- **Dissolving form**: Figurative elements are unstable and may be broken down by material at any moment.

## Emotion And Valence/Arousal

This direction often produces heaviness, pity, trauma, resistance, absurdity, bodily discomfort, or raw life force. Arousal is usually medium to high: rough materials and bodily hints raise tension, while low value and compressed space lower valence. If the image includes humor, bright color, or defiant irony, valence becomes more complex rather than simply negative.

In output, you might write: "The stucco-like surface and flattened bodily suggestion increase tactile discomfort; dark color and broken edges lower valence; but the rough-line figure carries anti-academic humor, so the work also has resistance and absurdity."

## Common Misreadings

- Do not equate Dubuffet's professional art directly with Art Brut; he was inspired by Art Brut but worked within a professional art context.
- The difference from Matter Painting is that this direction often contains clearer hints of bodies, faces, or anti-cultural figuration.
- When distinguishing it from pure Expressionism, look for Art Informel's material dissolution and postwar European context.
