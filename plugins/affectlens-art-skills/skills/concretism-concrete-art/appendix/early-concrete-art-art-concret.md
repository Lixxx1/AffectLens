# Early Concrete Art / Art Concret

## Definition and Historical Context

Early Concrete Art / Art Concret is closely tied to Theo van Doesburg's 1930 concept of "concrete art." MoMA's Concrete Art entry states van Doesburg's idea that nothing is more concrete or more real than line, color, and plane. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/concrete-art) This means Concrete Art is not abstracted from nature; it treats line, color, and plane themselves as the work's reality.

Early Art Concret rejects natural representation, symbolism, narrative, and subjective expression. It seeks non-objective, rational, clear, constructible art. It is adjacent to De Stijl, Constructivism, and geometric abstraction. National Galleries of Scotland's De Stijl resource can serve as a reference for vertical/horizontal structures, primary colors, and abstract harmony, while Britannica's Constructivism resource provides comparison for construction, modern materials, and social function. [National Galleries of Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/de-stijl-style) [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Constructivism-art) Concrete Art more strongly emphasizes that the work does not point to an external object and is composed only through formal relations.

## Visual Identification

- **Line, color, and plane are self-sufficient**: the work does not imply landscape, figure, object, or psychological image.
- **Clear geometry**: rectangles, straight lines, circles, triangles, and pure color blocks organize the image.
- **Impersonal surface**: little brushwork, clear boundaries, and a precisely designed appearance.
- **No symbolic narrative**: title and form usually do not guide the viewer toward an external story.
- **Rule-based relations**: proportion, repetition, direction, and color relation matter more than subject. Use line, shape, color, space, and composition as primary evidence. [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/formal_analysis.html)

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Early Concrete Art often creates coolness, rationality, stability, clarity, or mechanical order. Valence is usually neutral or positive because clear structure and balanced relations feel stable. Arousal is usually low to medium unless diagonals, high contrast, or dense repetition create visual stimulation. Emotion is not story-driven; it comes from proportion, color purity, and viewing rhythm.

Suggested wording:

```text
The red, blue, and yellow planes with vertical-horizontal structure create non-objective
order, making valence clear and stable. Strong contrast raises arousal slightly, but the
overall effect remains rational and low in activation.
```

## Misreading Warnings

- Do not call all geometric abstraction Concrete Art; Concrete Art more strongly excludes natural reference and symbolism.
- Distinguish it from Constructivism by checking for industrial, revolutionary, architectural, or social-function context.
- Distinguish it from De Stijl by noting that De Stijl has a more explicit utopian ideal of harmony, while Concrete Art emphasizes the reality of form itself.
