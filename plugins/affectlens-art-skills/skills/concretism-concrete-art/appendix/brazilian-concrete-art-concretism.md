# Brazilian Concrete Art / Concretism

## Definition and Historical Context

Brazilian Concrete Art / Concretism developed in the 1950s in the context of rapid industrialization, modern design, poetry, architecture, and geometric abstraction in Brazil. MoMA's Neo-Concrete Art entry explains that two Brazilian groups related to Concrete Art emerged in the 1950s: Grupo Ruptura in Sao Paulo and Grupo Frente in Rio de Janeiro. These artists moved away from both abstraction and figuration toward geometric works focused on principles of color, line, and shape. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/neo-concrete-art)

Brazilian Concrete Art often emphasizes modernization, rational order, geometric systems, flat design, and the social modernity of visual language. MoMA's Concrete Art definition helps judge its painting and design side through self-sufficient line, color, and plane. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/concrete-art) Britannica's Concrete Poetry entry helps explain how Brazilian Concretism extended into text, page layout, and poetic structure. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/concrete-poetry) It also provides the basis for the later Neo-Concrete reaction, with Oiticica's *Parangoles* serving as a key later reference for the shift from rational geometry toward body, audience, and experiential space. [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/art-1010/latin-america-modernism/brazilian-modernism/a/helio-oiticica-parangoles)

## Visual Identification

- **Clear geometric system**: lines, colors, planes, modules, grids, and repetition are legible.
- **Modern design feeling**: works may resemble posters, architecture, logos, concrete poetry layouts, or public visual order.
- **Self-sufficient color and shape**: the work does not depict natural objects and emphasizes visual principles.
- **Rational and industrial tone**: precise structure, little gesture, and an urban-modern design order.
- **Cross-media possibility**: painting, print, design, poetry, and architecture may influence each other.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Brazilian Concretism often feels modern, clear, optimistic, rational, and constructive. Valence is usually positive or neutral because it is tied to modernization and order. Arousal depends on color strength, geometric density, diagonals, and rotation. Regular grids reduce tension; bright color and dynamic geometry raise activation.

Suggested wording:

```text
The clear modules and industrialized geometry make valence feel modern and constructive.
High-saturation color and diagonal repetition raise arousal, while the overall system
remains closer to Brazilian Concretism than lyrical abstraction.
```

## Misreading Warnings

- Do not classify every Brazilian geometric abstraction as Neo-Concrete. If the work remains closed, flat, rational, and nonparticipatory, it may be closer to Concrete Art.
- Distinguish it from Constructivism: Brazilian Concretism does not necessarily emphasize revolutionary production or Soviet industrial context.
- Distinguish it from Op Art: it does not necessarily aim at optical illusion.
