# De Stijl / Neo-Plasticism Adjacent Direction

## Definition and Historical Context

De Stijl / Neo-plasticism is one of the important adjacent sources for Concrete Art. National Galleries of Scotland explains that De Stijl was promoted through a journal and artist group founded by Theo van Doesburg in 1917, including Mondrian, Bart van der Leck, Georges Vantongerloo, and others; they sought universal harmony through horizontal and vertical lines, right angles, flat primary colors, and a limited visual vocabulary. [National Galleries of Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/de-stijl-style)

The same source notes that van Doesburg later founded Concrete Art in 1930. MoMA's Concrete Art entry helps clarify his later position that line, color, and plane are real in themselves. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/concrete-art) De Stijl and Concrete Art therefore have historical continuity, but De Stijl carries a stronger postwar ideal of rebuilding order, utopia, and spiritual harmony. MoMA's De Stijl entry is useful for reading its abstract geometry, design expansion, and modernist idealism. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/de-stijl)

## Visual Identification

- **Horizontal and vertical structure**: black lines, right angles, rectangles, and grids are core grammar.
- **Primary colors plus black, white, and gray**: red, yellow, and blue often appear with white grounds and black lines.
- **Asymmetrical balance**: the work may not be centered, but balance is achieved through color-area size and placement. Analyze line, shape, color, space, and composition as formal evidence. [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/formal_analysis.html)
- **Flatness**: traditional perspective and volume are rejected in favor of two-dimensional relations.
- **Design expansion**: architecture, furniture, page layout, and interiors may use related language.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

De Stijl / Neo-plasticism often evokes order, balance, clarity, rationality, and idealism. Valence is usually positive or neutral because the image seeks harmony. Arousal is usually low, though strong primary colors, black-line division, and asymmetry create moderate visual activity. Compared with cooler Concrete Art, De Stijl may carry a clearer spiritual wish for reconstruction and balance.

Suggested wording:

```text
Horizontal and vertical black lines with primary-color rectangles create stable order and
lower arousal. Asymmetrical color blocks keep the eye moving, while overall valence
leans rational, harmonious, and positive.
```

## Misreading Warnings

- Do not call every red-yellow-blue geometric work De Stijl; the key evidence is horizontal/vertical structure, primary colors with black-white-gray, and the ideal of harmony.
- Diagonals may suggest van Doesburg's Elementarism or another geometric abstraction.
- When distinguishing it from Concrete Art, explain whether the work is closer to De Stijl's utopian order or to Concrete Art's self-sufficient formal reality.
