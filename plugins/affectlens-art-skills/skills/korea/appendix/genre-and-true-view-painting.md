# Genre and True-View Painting

Genre painting and true-view landscape are important directions for understanding late Joseon Korean painting emotion. Genre painters such as Kim Hongdo and Shin Yunbok depicted labor, leisure, markets, drinking parties, games, gender relations, and everyday life. The Met's *Work and Leisure* resource notes that 18th-century Korean genre painting centered everyday activity and became an important part of Korean painting history. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/work-and-leisure-eighteenth-century-genre-painting-in-korea) True-view landscape painters such as Jeong Seon turned specific Korean scenic sites into painting subjects; the Met's Korean landscape essay is useful for this Koreanized landscape tradition. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/mountain-and-water-korean-landscape-painting-1400-1800)

To identify genre painting, look for people in recognizable life scenes: work, shoe making, gambling, drinking, dance viewing, study rooms, riverside gatherings, street scenes, or market life. National Museum of Korea-related research can supplement the social-life and late-Joseon context of Korean genre painting. [National Museum of Korea](https://www.ijkaa.org/v.3/0/68/85) Lines are usually concise and lively, with more active posture and expression than in landscape painting. To identify true-view landscape, look for specific Korean places such as the Diamond Mountains, named sites, travel paths, and a sense of local identity.

Emotionally, genre painting often has positive valence and medium arousal. Humorous action, multiple people interacting, and everyday stories create closeness, human warmth, lightness, or satire. If scenes include labor, poverty, or hierarchy, valence becomes more complex. True-view landscape tends toward clarity, nostalgia, grandeur, or local pride; arousal depends on mountain force and brushwork. Specific place makes the emotion not only "nature calm" but also memory and cultural belonging.

It is easily confused with Chinese genre painting and Japanese ukiyo-e. Korean genre painting has distinct Joseon clothing, social context, spatial handling, and humor. Ukiyo-e more often has print-like flatness, strong cropping, and Edo urban entertainment context. True-view landscape should not be treated as generic Chinese landscape, because site specificity and Koreanization are key evidence.
