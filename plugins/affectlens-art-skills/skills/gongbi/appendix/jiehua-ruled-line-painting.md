# Jiehua / Ruled-Line Painting

Jiehua is a Chinese painting mode that uses ruler-assisted line to depict architecture, pavilions, bridges, vehicles, vessels, objects, and spatial structures. It is closely related to gongbi's fine line and precision. Britannica's gongbi entry mentions jiehua in relation to meticulous architectural depiction. It is often translated as "ruled-line painting," though the Chinese term preserves its tool-based and traditional context. The Nottingham ePrints study *Painting Architecture: Jiehua in Yuan China* is a useful reference for ruled lines, precise architectural structures, and Yuan-period research.

## Recognition

First check whether architectural lines are unusually stable, straight, and precise. Terraces, towers, palaces, railings, eaves, beams, steps, bridges, boats, and object edges may show many parallel lines, right angles, diagonal structures, and dense construction detail. Jiehua does not necessarily follow Western single-point perspective. It often combines Chinese handscroll space, segmented narrative, and moving viewpoint. Figures, trees, rocks, and mountains may serve as supplements to the built environment.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Jiehua often creates order, solemnity, precision, courtliness, or narrative depth. Denser architecture, more lines, and more complex spatial layers raise arousal. Even lines and symmetrical composition stabilize emotion. Palaces and ritual scenes can create dignified positive or neutral valence; empty towers, cold color, distant views, or uninhabited structures can create coolness, nostalgia, or solitude. Its emotion comes from structural density and layered viewing paths, not from liberated brushwork.

## Common Confusion

Jiehua may be confused with architectural drawing, printmaking, or Western perspective painting. Although precise, it remains part of Chinese painting's linear, scroll-based, narrative spatial system, not an engineering blueprint. It may overlap with court painting, blue-green landscape, or historical narrative painting. In classification, check whether architecture is organized by fine ruled line, whether the medium is ink/color on paper or silk, and whether space follows Chinese viewing rhythm.
