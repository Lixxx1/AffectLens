# Baimiao Line Drawing

Baimiao is a Chinese painting technique that outlines subjects with ink line, usually with no color or very little color. It can appear in figure, bird-and-flower, Buddhist, and narrative painting. The Palace Museum's collection entry for Ma Zheng's baimiao figure handscroll can serve as a reference for baimiao figure imagery and ink-line modeling. Baimiao is closely related to gongbi because gongbi often begins from baimiao-like line work, but baimiao itself emphasizes the independent aesthetic of line.

## Recognition

Do not identify baimiao only by fineness. The key is whether the image almost entirely relies on line to build form, structure, and feeling. Drapery, hair, clouds, flowers, faces, hands, and object edges are all carried by line; if color appears, it is usually minimal. Lines may be rounded, threadlike, paused, flying, or steady. Traditional classifications such as the "eighteen line methods" describe different approaches to drapery and form. When viewing, describe line speed, thickness, turns, continuity, and rhythm.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Baimiao usually has lower arousal than heavy-color gongbi. It feels literary, restrained, pale, refined, and airy. Even fine lines and large empty space reduce visual stimulation, producing neutral, clear, or calm valence. If lines are tense, figures twisted, or religious/historical subjects heavy, valence may become solemn or sorrowful. Baimiao's emotion comes from the breathing of line rather than color: the smoother the line, the gentler the image; the tighter, sharper, or more broken the line, the more tension appears.

## Common Confusion

Baimiao can be confused with Western sketching or unfinished gongbi drafts. Western drawing often builds space through shading, hatching, and volume; baimiao emphasizes contour, drapery, and calligraphic line, and does not necessarily pursue optical volume. An unfinished gongbi draft may look like baimiao, but if it is clearly only a preparatory step before coloring, use title and completion evidence. Baimiao can be a complete aesthetic form, not merely an uncolored incomplete work.
