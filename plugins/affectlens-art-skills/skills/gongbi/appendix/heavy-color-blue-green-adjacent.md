# Heavy Color / Blue-Green Adjacent Direction

This direction refers to gongbi-related works that use strong mineral pigments, azurite, malachite, cinnabar, ochre, gold powder, or highly decorative color. Britannica's definition of gongbi supports the connection between meticulous brushwork and coloring traditions. The direction can appear in figures, court scenes, religious images, bird-and-flower painting, and landscape, overlapping with blue-green landscape, academic painting, Buddhist painting, and court decoration. It is not simply "bright color"; it uses layered color, mineral-pigment texture, and line structure to create richness, solemnity, or sacredness.

## Recognition

Look for color that is heavy and bright while still controlled by line. Azurite and malachite often create cool but gorgeous rock, mountain, or garment surfaces; cinnabar, gold, and ochre may appear in clothing, ornament, religious halos, or court objects. Unlike oil impasto, heavy-color gongbi usually keeps a relatively flat surface, with color governed by contour and wash. Gold-and-blue pavilions, rich landscapes, sumptuous clothing, Buddhist figures, court birds, or auspicious images may suggest this direction. The National Palace Museum's *The Three Friends and a Hundred Birds* can serve as a reference for courtly auspicious combinations and dense coloring.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Heavy color and blue-green approaches usually raise arousal more than pale-color gongbi. Saturated mineral color, gold, dense pattern, and abundant detail can create luxury, solemnity, celebration, sacredness, or ritual atmosphere. If color is too dense, figures numerous, or architecture complex, the image may feel oppressive or dazzling. Valence is often positive or solemn-neutral, though religious, historical, or farewell subjects can complicate it. Blue-green color with empty space can feel cool, immortal, or elevated; gold and red feel warmer, richer, and more ceremonial.

## Common Confusion

This direction can be confused with New Year prints, decorative illustration, or blue-green landscape. New Year prints often have stronger folk print character, more simplified shapes, and direct festival symbols. Heavy-color gongbi has finer contour and layered coloring. If the image is mainly a landscape with little figure or flower-bird content, classify it first as blue-green landscape rather than gongbi figure/bird-and-flower.
