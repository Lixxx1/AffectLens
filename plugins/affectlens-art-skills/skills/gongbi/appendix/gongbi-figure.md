# Gongbi Figure Painting

Gongbi figure painting centers on fine line, precise form, facial detail, clothing rhythm, and narrative setting. Britannica's gongbi entry emphasizes meticulous brush technique and its relation to figure and narrative subjects. It can depict beauties, emperors, scholars, historical figures, Daoist and Buddhist figures, court life, opera, and literary stories. Unlike Western portraiture based on volumetric light and shadow, gongbi figure painting relies more on line, layered color, costume pattern, posture, and relationship to establish character.

## Recognition

Begin with line. Are facial contours, hair strands, eyebrows, eyes, fingers, collars, cuffs, belts, and drapery organized by steady fine lines? Drapery lines not only describe clothing; they establish posture, status, and emotion. Rounded, even, continuous lines make figures feel dignified and restrained; fine lines with pauses and turns give posture more life. Costume patterns, headdress, hair, objects, and background are also evidence of identity and narrative, so do not read only facial expression.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Emotion should combine posture, gaze, and color. A lowered head, turned body, hanging sleeves, distant gaze, fan, or flower can produce reserve, melancholy, elegance, or solitude. Front-facing seated posture, ritual formality, gold, or heavy-colored clothing creates dignity, richness, and medium-to-high arousal. Pale color, blank space, and soft line lower arousal; dense figures, complex clothing, and strong color raise it. Valence depends strongly on subject: court women can feel graceful or secluded; religious figures can feel solemn, compassionate, or mysterious.

## Common Confusion

The main confusions are baimiao, New Year prints, and Western realist figures. If the work is almost only ink line with little color, it may lean baimiao. If color is flat and bold, festival symbols direct, and print texture obvious, it may lean New Year print. If modeling, light-shadow volume, and focal perspective dominate, it may be closer to Western realism. Gongbi figure painting depends on line, color, paper/silk material, costume pattern, and Chinese narrative context together. MSU Denver CVA's contemporary gongbi exhibition can also serve as a reference for continuation of gongbi line in modern figure and narrative work.
