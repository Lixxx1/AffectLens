# Machines / Self-Destroying Machines

## Definition And Context

The machines subtype is mainly associated with Jean Tinguely. It combines wheels, motors, metal rods, scrap, instruments, noise, and unstable movement into mechanical works, sometimes ending in self-destruction. MoMA's Fragment from Homage to New York can be used as a concrete reference for Tinguely's self-destroying machine event and surviving remains. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81174) The point is not only the static remnant, but the time-based experience of operation, sound, malfunction, loss of control, and destruction.

## Visual Identification

In photographs, look for wheels, belts, motors, rods, old machine parts, loose joints, and traces of movement. Tinguely-like machines often look temporary, chaotic, clumsy, and performative rather than stable industrial design. MoMA Through Time's Self-Destructing Art resource can serve as a reference for smoke, noise, audience, failure, and destruction as the work. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/interactives/moma_through_time/1960/self-destructing-art/) If documentation includes smoke, noise, sparks, audience, remains, or fragments after destruction, include event process in the analysis.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Arousal is usually very high. Motion, noise, collision, failure, loss of control, and self-destruction bring tension, absurdity, danger, and anticipation. Valence can be positive or negative: absurd machine action may feel humorous or playful, while scrap, malfunction, destruction, and technological loss of control can feel anxious or apocalyptic. Static remains should be read through the context that they once moved and were destroyed.

## Common Misreadings

Do not classify all kinetic art as Nouveau Realisme machines. Tinguely-like machines emphasize scrap, temporary construction, absurd movement, and satire of the machine age. Centre Pompidou's New Realism resource can serve as a boundary reference for real objects, waste, and urban / industrial experience entering art. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/programme/agenda/evenement/c9nBEXa) If the work pursues precise light effects, geometric order, or pure visual movement, it may be kinetic art, Op Art, or lumino-kinetic art rather than this subtype.
