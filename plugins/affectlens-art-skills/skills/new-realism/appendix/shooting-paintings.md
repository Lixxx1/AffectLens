# Shooting Paintings

## Definition And Context

Shooting Paintings, or Tirs, are mainly associated with Niki de Saint Phalle. MoMA's artist page can be used as a basic reference for her place in modern and contemporary art. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/1444) The artist hid bags of pigment behind plaster or a white surface and shot them so pigment burst and dripped. Guggenheim Bilbao's "Rifle Art" resource can be used as a concrete reference for shooting, pigment rupture, and the Tirs process. [Guggenheim Bilbao](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibition/arte-a-tiros) The work turns painting from hand application into public action, violent trigger, and chance generation, connecting Nouveau Realisme, performance, body politics, and challenges to painting authority.

## Visual Identification

The surface often shows white or pale plaster, bullet holes, cracks, sprayed pigment, drips, and colorful wounds after rupture. Unlike Abstract Expressionist pouring, the traces come from shooting and hidden pigment bags. Guggenheim's Niki de Saint Phalle exhibition resource can serve as a reference for combining shooting action, surface damage, and painting generation. [Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/niki-de-saint-phalle) If documentation includes audience participation, guns, targets, exploding pigment, or plaster surfaces, include the action process in the judgment.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Arousal is very high because gunshots, rupture, holes, and dripping directly imply impact. Valence is mixed: violence and wound-like surfaces lower valence, while pigment release, rebellion, and participatory ritual may bring liberation, pleasure, or frenzy. Bright color alone should not be read as positive; consider bullet holes, attack, broken white surfaces, and social gender / power critique.

## Common Misreadings

Do not classify a work as Abstract Expressionism only because it has splashes. Shooting Paintings require shooting action, bullet holes, hidden pigment bags, and rupture event. National Galleries of Scotland's New Realism entry can serve as a reference for real action, material, and chance event entering the movement context. [National Galleries of Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/new-realism) If there is no shooting or action trace and only dripping pigment, it is more likely action painting or general abstraction.
