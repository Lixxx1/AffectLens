# Tableaux-Pieges

## Definition And Context

Tableaux-pieges, often translated as "trap pictures," are associated with Daniel Spoerri. Tate's Nouveau realisme resource can serve as a movement reference for Spoerri and other artists who incorporated real objects and everyday remains directly into art. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/n/nouveau-realisme) These works fix dining tables, plates, cups, food remains, cigarette butts, utensils, and accidental leftovers in place, then often display the tabletop vertically. Unlike still-life painting, they capture a real daily site, freezing time, social activity, consumption, and bodily traces.

## Visual Identification

Look for a tabletop turned upright: plates, cups, knives, forks, bottles, bread crumbs, napkins, ash, and food residue fixed on a plane, sometimes contradicting gravity. Centre Pompidou's New Realism exhibition resource can serve as a reference for real everyday objects, consumer traces, and accidental sites entering art. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/programme/agenda/evenement/c9nBEXa) The objects are not elegantly arranged; they preserve after-meal disorder, accidental positions, and traces of use. The work often feels as if someone has just left.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Valence easily oscillates. Familiar tableware and meal traces can bring intimacy, humor, or social memory; leftovers, dirt, cigarette butts, and fixed decay lower valence through disgust, absurdity, or the shadow of stopped time. Arousal is often medium: the work is not explosive, but the reversal of gravity and daily objects "pinned" in place create sustained unease. Dense or visibly dirty remains raise arousal.

## Common Misreadings

Do not treat tableaux-pieges as ordinary still life or table installation. The key is that they preserve and flip a real site rather than depicting a table. MoMA's The New Realists portfolio can serve as a reference for Spoerri's shared Nouveau Realisme context. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/portfolios/66202) If a work only paints food and tableware, it is not a trap picture; if a staged table lacks the logic of capturing an accidental site, be cautious.
