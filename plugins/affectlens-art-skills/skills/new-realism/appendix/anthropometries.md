# Anthropometries

## Definition And Context

Anthropometries are Yves Klein's series using the human body as a painting tool. Female models applied pigment to their bodies and left imprints on paper or canvas. Centre Pompidou's ANT 82, Anthropometrie de l'epoque bleue is a concrete reference for body contact, blue pigment, and performative imprint. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/offre-aux-professionnels/enseignants/dossiers-ressources-sur-lart/yves-klein/ant-82-anthropometrie-de-lepoque-bleue) Klein called bodies "living brushes," and Pierre Restany used the term Anthropometrie. This subtype crosses body, performance, and material trace within Nouveau Realisme and relates to Klein's International Klein Blue, monochromes, and ritualized performances.

## Visual Identification

Images often show blue body silhouettes, torso or limb imprints, and relatively empty backgrounds. The body appears as direct contact evidence rather than anatomical depiction. Edges may show pressure, dragging, and changes in pigment density. Tate's Nouveau realisme resource can serve as a movement context for real bodies, action, and material traces entering art. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/n/nouveau-realisme) Strong pure blue, bodily contact traces, performance-record feeling, and blank space all point toward Anthropometries rather than ordinary body abstraction or blue monochrome.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Valence is usually mixed. Pure blue and blank space can bring stillness, spirituality, and even positive transcendence; the body used as a tool and fixed as an imprint can also feel provocative, objectifying, or uneasy. Arousal depends on imprint intensity and action trace: clear body contours, dragging marks, and performance context raise arousal; centered monochrome-like imprints with ample blank space feel cooler. Analysis should include both the spiritual color effect and the bodily event.

## Common Misreadings

Do not reduce Anthropometries to "blue bodies." Look for evidence that the body produced the mark through contact. Centre Pompidou's New Realism resource can be used as a broader reference for bodies, objects, real traces, and actions in the movement. [Centre Pompidou](https://mediation.centrepompidou.fr/education/ressources/ENS-newrea-EN/ENS-newrea-EN.htm) If there is only a blue monochrome without body traces, it is closer to Klein's monochrome; if it is traditional nude representation, it is not this subtype.
