# Hanover / Merz

Hanover Dada usually centers on Kurt Schwitters and Merz. MoMA explains Merz as Schwitters's term for collage and assemblage made from salvaged discarded materials. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/merz) Merz materials include tickets, waste paper, packaging, cloth scraps, wood pieces, advertising fragments, printed letters, and urban debris. Unlike Berlin Dada's political photomontage, Merz emphasizes how everyday fragments can be transformed into formal order and poetic structure.

When identifying Merz, first ask whether the material comes from ordinary life and carries traces of use. Train tickets, receipts, labels, newspapers, cloth edges, torn paper, and printed scraps may be arranged into flat compositions or shallow assemblages. Philadelphia Museum of Art's *Merz Construction* and Peggy Guggenheim Collection's *Merz Drawing 75* are useful references for materials and construction methods. [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://www.visitpham.org/objects/53863) [Peggy Guggenheim Collection](https://www.guggenheim-venice.it/en/art/works/merz-drawing-75/) The surface may look discarded and chance-based, yet often contains careful balance, rhythm, and color-block relationships. Schwitters's Merz is not just a trash pile; it transforms modern urban remnants into a new abstract structure and private universe.

Emotionally, Merz is often lower-key than Berlin Dada. Waste paper, old tickets, and damaged edges can create nostalgia, debris, poverty, or traces of postwar life, making valence complex. At the same time, formal order, color balance, and material rhythm can suggest repair, wit, or quiet reconstruction. Arousal depends on fragment density and material contrast: restrained color and stable layout lower arousal; many cut directions, dense text, and strong texture conflicts raise it. Its aesthetic often comes from the subtle transformation of discarded matter into order.

The main confusions are Synthetic Cubist collage and abstract construction. Synthetic Cubist collage usually still revolves around object structures such as guitar, bottle, or tabletop. Merz more often builds nonrepresentational or semi-abstract order from urban debris. Constructivism emphasizes engineering and social construction; Merz is more personal, fragmentary, and marked by everyday residue. Judge whether the materials have discarded origins, whether they form Schwitters-like collage or assemblage logic, and whether emotion arises between brokenness and order.
