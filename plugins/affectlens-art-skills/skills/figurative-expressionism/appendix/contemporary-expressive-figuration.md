# Broad Contemporary Expressive Figuration

Broad contemporary expressive figuration is an appreciation category rather than a strict historical movement. Contemporary painters often use rough brushwork, distortion, impasto, non-natural color, and psychological space while keeping recognizable people, bodies, interiors, homes, streets, or landscapes. Tate's Figurative Art entry supports the basic judgment that recognizable people or objects remain central. This category inherits from Expressionism, Abstract Expressionism, Bay Area Figuration, Neo-expressionism, and contemporary figurative painting, but it does not necessarily belong to any fixed group.

## Recognition

First confirm that the figurative core is clear enough: figures, bodies, or scenes remain recognizable, and emotion is carried by both image and painterly handling. If the image contains only color fields and marks, it is more abstract; if figures are highly realist and brushwork hidden, it is closer to contemporary realism. Expressive figuration gives figures psychological intensity through color, edge, paint thickness, and spatial distortion. MoMA's Action Painting entry can help explain how gesture, body movement, and painting process enter image emotion.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Avoid equating rough brushwork with intensity by default. Warm color, relaxed posture, and open space may produce tenderness, intimacy, or humor. Cool color, closed space, twisted bodies, and heavy contour may produce loneliness, anxiety, or pressure. Arousal is shaped by brush speed, contrast, saturation, and bodily distortion; valence is shaped by expression, posture, spatial relation, and color temperature. If faces are unclear, rely more on posture, distance, and pictorial pressure. Getty's formal analysis framework can support these judgments through line, shape, color, space, and composition.

## Common Confusion

The main confusion is Neo-expressionism. If a work clearly belongs to a 1980s context with large-scale historical symbols, myth, politics, and aggressive materiality, it may be Neo-expressionist. If it is simply a contemporary painter using rough brushwork on figures, "broad contemporary expressive figuration" is safer. Also distinguish from illustration-like rough style: true expressive figuration should make brushwork, color, and distortion carry emotion, not merely function as surface effect.
