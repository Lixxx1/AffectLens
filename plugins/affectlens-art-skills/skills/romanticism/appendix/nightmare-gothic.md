# Nightmare / Gothic

The Gothic direction in Romanticism turns fear, sleep, death, ruins, ghosts, confinement, and supernatural suggestion into visual drama. It may include dark rooms, moonlight, monsters, demons, graveyards, ruined architecture, or vulnerable bodies.

Valence is usually negative or mixed, while arousal is often high because the scene stages threat, suspense, or psychological disturbance. Low light and stillness can create slow dread; violent gesture or apparition raises arousal sharply.

Distinguish it from Surrealism by historical context and Gothic terror rather than twentieth-century unconscious dream logic.
