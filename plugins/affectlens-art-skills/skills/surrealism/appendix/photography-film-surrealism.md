# Photography / Film Direction

## Definition and Context

Photography and film gave Surrealism another way to enter dream and the unconscious. Because these media appear to record reality, they can turn apparent evidence into dream logic and irrational association. Multiple exposure, editing, displaced bodies, shadows, mirrors, montage, close-up fragments, and discontinuous narrative can all make real images feel uncanny.

Compared with painting, this direction often carries the special unease of "reality evidence" being distorted.

## Image Identification

Look for body parts cut off or recombined, faces obscured, mirrored doubling, misplaced shadows, discontinuous space, abnormal scale, and scenes that resemble a single frame from a dream. In film, examine whether editing breaks causality or links people, eyes, knives, doors, animals, or machines through irrational association.

The clearer the photographic detail, the stronger the uncanny effect can become, because the viewer oscillates between visible fact and dream impossibility.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

This direction is often medium to high arousal, especially when bodies are fragmented, eyes are targeted, shadows behave strangely, or editing is abrupt. Valence is often mixed or negative: soft dream light can feel poetic, but bodily displacement, black-and-white contrast, and violent implication create unease.

Explain how photographic realism intensifies the Surrealist effect instead of treating the work as ordinary experimental imagery.

## Misclassification Warning

Not all experimental photography is Surrealist. Look for dreams, the unconscious, chance encounter, body displacement, or anti-rational logic. Compared with Expressionist film, Surrealist film emphasizes free association and discontinuous dream logic rather than only dramatic set design or expressive lighting.
