# Object / Assemblage Direction

## Definition and Context

The object and assemblage direction uses found objects, everyday things, boxes, body parts, furniture, toys, machines, and mismatched materials to make familiar objects feel strange, erotic, comic, or threatening. It inherits Dada's challenge through readymades, but its emphasis is more psychological: desire, dream association, chance encounter, and the transformation of everyday reality by the unconscious.

Objects are not only materials here. They act as psychological triggers.

## Image Identification

Look for ordinary objects placed in the wrong function, scale, or environment: a cup covered in fur, a telephone turned into a living thing, furniture behaving like a body, or machines carrying erotic or violent implications. The work may be sculpture, box construction, installation, collage, or a photographic record.

The key is not object use alone, but irrational relationship. The object should activate dreamlike, eroticized, or unconscious association. If the work is only decorative accumulation, it may not be Surrealist.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Valence is often mixed. Humor, wit, and play can lean positive, while bodily implication, hair, sharp objects, taboo material, and broken function may produce disgust, anxiety, or desire. Arousal depends on how intimate, bodily, or functionally wrong the object relationships are.

Avoid saying only that the work is "strange." Name which ordinary objects are estranged and how that estrangement creates unconscious association.

## Misclassification Warning

This direction overlaps with Dada, Nouveau Realisme, and assemblage. Dada emphasizes anti-art and satirical destruction; Nouveau Realisme emphasizes real objects from consumer society; Surrealist objects emphasize dream, desire, the unconscious, and psychological transformation.
