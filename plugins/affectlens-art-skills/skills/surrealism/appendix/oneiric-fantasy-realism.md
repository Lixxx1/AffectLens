# Oneiric Surrealism

## Definition and Context

Oneiric or fantasy-realist Surrealism uses precise, cool, often classical technique to depict impossible dream scenes. It is a major Surrealist method for placing dreams, the unconscious, and the surface of reality side by side. Its typical features are realist light, deep perspective, crisp edges, empty horizons, and abnormal object combinations that make irrational scenes appear convincing.

Artists such as Dali, Magritte, and Tanguy are useful references because they present impossible or dreamlike relationships through clear visual language.

## Image Identification

Look for melting, floating, enlarging, displacement, substitution, and broken scale in ordinary objects. Rooms may contain skies, bodies may become furniture, stones may hang in the air, or shadows may point in impossible directions. The space is often quiet and deep; edges are clear; brushwork is restrained.

The more photographic and convincing the surface appears, the stronger the shock of the impossible relationship. If an image is merely a fantasy world with coherent narrative rules, it is not necessarily Surrealist. Look for dream logic, rupture, and unconscious association.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

This direction usually produces medium to high arousal because the viewer keeps discovering broken rules. Valence is often mixed: humor, wonder, and play may lean positive, while loneliness, empty space, fragmented bodies, death imagery, and cold shadows lower valence.

The central emotional evidence is the clash between a believable surface and an impossible combination.

## Misclassification Warning

This direction is easily confused with Magic Realism and fantasy illustration. Magic Realism tends to absorb the marvelous into an everyday narrative; oneiric Surrealism emphasizes dream rupture and unconscious shock. Fantasy illustration often builds a coherent world; Surrealism makes meaning unstable.
