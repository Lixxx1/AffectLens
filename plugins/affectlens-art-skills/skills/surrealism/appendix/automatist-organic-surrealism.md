# Automatist / Organic Surrealism

## Definition and Context

Automatist or organic Surrealism emphasizes automatism: bypassing rational control so that lines, shapes, words, chance traces, and games can draw out unconscious association. It does not need to look like Dali's precise realism. It may be more abstract, fluid, nervous, vegetal, insect-like, cartographic, or embryonic.

Automatic drawing by artists such as Masson and organic signs by Miro are useful references. This direction belongs to Surrealism's wider exploration of dreams, the unconscious, and chance process.

## Image Identification

Look for lines that seem to wander unconsciously, semi-abstract or semi-biological forms, unstable boundaries, and marks produced through doodling, collage, frottage, grattage, fumage, random arrangement, or game-like procedures. The composition may not have a central narrative; the viewing path follows chains of association rather than traditional story space.

If the image appears abstract but suggests dreams, bodies, sexuality, animals, plants, or unconscious formation, it may belong to this direction.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal can be high or low. Soft curves, floating forms, and open space can create dreaminess, play, and contemplation. Dense lines, sharpness, rupture, and biological forms raise arousal and may create unease or desire.

Valence is often open: the image can feel light, strange, playful, or troubling. Use line speed, density, and bodily association as evidence.

## Misclassification Warning

Do not treat every abstract doodle as automatist Surrealism. There should be evidence of unconscious process, dream association, automatic creation, or Surrealist strategy. Compared with Abstract Expressionism, this direction is earlier and more tied to psychology, dream, and automatic procedure.
