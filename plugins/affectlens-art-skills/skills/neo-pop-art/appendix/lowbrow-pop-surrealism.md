# Lowbrow / Pop Surrealism

Lowbrow / Pop Surrealism combines comics, underground culture, hot rods, tiki, punk, toys, horror films, cartoons, tattoos, street imagery, and surreal fantasy. Oceanside Museum of Art and the Aldrich Contemporary Art Museum connect it to pop culture, surrealism, and low-culture sources historically excluded by traditional art institutions.

## Recognition

Look for highly finished cartoon figures, grotesque cute creatures, vintage advertising, monsters, dreamscapes, dark humor, candy color, horror, and playful weirdness. Salt Lake Community College's Lowbrow Art exhibition material can serve as a reference for the combination of low culture, popular imagery, cartoonization, and anti-academic taste. Lowbrow / Pop Surrealism is usually more narrative, underground, and fantastical than Neo-Pop; more West Coast subcultural than Superflat; and closer to commercial illustration, comics, and pop spectacle than traditional Surrealism.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Lowbrow / Pop Surrealism is often cute and unsettling at the same time. Bright color, rounded characters, and nostalgic imagery create positive valence; grotesque bodies, dark humor, and nightmare elements lower valence or raise arousal. Aldrich's Pop Surrealism context supports reading popular imagery, surreal fantasy, and contemporary art together rather than dismissing it as "just illustration."
