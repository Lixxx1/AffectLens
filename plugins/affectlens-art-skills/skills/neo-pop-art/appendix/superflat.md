# Superflat

Superflat is a Japanese contemporary art concept and movement proposed by Takashi Murakami. It draws from manga, anime, otaku culture, Edo visual traditions, commercial characters, and extremely flattened imagery. The Art Story's Superflat entry is a useful reference for the movement, Murakami's concept, and Japanese popular culture context. "Flatness" refers both to visual flatness and to the collapsing of high/low culture, consumer culture, and image hierarchies. Takashi Murakami, Yoshitomo Nara, Chiho Aoshima, and Aya Takano are often discussed in this context.

## Recognition

Look for anime-like eyes, cute characters, flat color, clean outlines, repeated patterns, flowers, monsters, girls, consumer packaging, and commercial collaboration. Gagosian's Takashi Murakami artist page can serve as a reference for flowers, characters, collaborations, and contemporary Japanese art-market context. Space is usually shallow; images sit on the surface; colors are bright and non-naturalistic. Superflat is not ordinary anime illustration. It self-consciously discusses Japanese postwar consumer culture, the art market, and image flatness.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Superflat often has high surface valence and high arousal: cute, sweet, bright, exciting, and entertaining. However, beneath the cute shell it may contain emptiness, anxiety, consumer fatigue, desire, or grotesque unease. Judge by character expression, repetition density, color sweetness, and whether unsettling elements appear.
