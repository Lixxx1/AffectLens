# Northern Mannerism

Northern Mannerism refers to the spread and transformation of Mannerism in the Netherlands, Flanders, Germany, Prague, and related regions. The Art Story's overview of Northern Mannerism is useful for regional transmission, print dissemination, and northern variants. [The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/northern-mannerism/) It often spread through prints, court collections, and artist travel, absorbing Italian elongated bodies and complex compositions while adding northern detail, allegory, strange architecture, landscape, and precise surfaces. Britannica's Mannerism entry provides the broader background of elongated proportion, complex pose, and artificial style. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Mannerism)

To identify it, look for extreme allegorical complexity, muscular nudes, twisting crowds, strange architecture, intricate ornament, and print-like linework. Northern works may feel denser, colder, and more bizarre than Italian Mannerism, as if figures were placed inside a maze of learning and collection. Rudolf II's Prague court can serve as a reference for the intersection of collecting, alchemy, court science, and unusual artistic taste in Northern Mannerism. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rupr/hd_rupr.htm) Mythology, alchemy, seasons, the five senses, morality, and courtly riddles are common.

Emotionally, Northern Mannerism often has relatively high arousal because detail is dense, poses multiply, and allegory is complex. Valence may feel intellectual, luxurious, strange, mysterious, or uneasy. Images with odd animals, dense bodies, and strong diagonals feel more tense; images built from precise allegory and cold light feel more rational and distanced.
