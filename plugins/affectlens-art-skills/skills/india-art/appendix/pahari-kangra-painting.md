# Pahari / Kangra Painting

Pahari painting refers to Indian miniature traditions from the small hill states in the Himalayan foothills. Kangra painting is often treated as one of its important lyrical and softened directions. Britannica places Pahari style within South Asian hill-state miniature painting; its subjects overlap with Rajput painting, but it often gives more weight to Krishna-Radha love, natural setting, seasons, hills, and poetic emotion. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/South-Asian-arts/Pahari-style) Kangra works are especially associated with soft tone, elegant line, and love themes.

To identify it, look for hill slopes, soft trees, riverbanks, pavilions, gardens, rain clouds, moonlit nights, Krishna and Radha, attendants, music, waiting, and mutual gazes. Princeton University Art Museum's Indian miniature painting resources can serve as a broad reference for the coexistence of figures, mythology, court life, and nature in Indian miniatures. [Princeton University Art Museum](https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/asian-art/south-asia/indian-miniature-paintings) Figures and line are usually gentler than in many Rajasthani works. Blue-green, pale pink, soft yellow, and night color often shape the mood. Space remains flattened, but nature acts like a lyrical stage that carries season and emotion.

Emotionally, Pahari/Kangra painting often has low to medium arousal and delicate valence. Lovers looking at one another, separation, monsoon, night, and hill landscapes can create tenderness, longing, pensiveness, poetic mood, sacred love, or sweet melancholy. Bright gardens and divine meetings may lean positive. Storm clouds, lightning, running poses, and deep blue-black atmosphere raise arousal and make valence more complex. Treat season, cloud, water, figure distance, and gesture as emotion evidence.

The main confusion is with Rajasthani and broader Rajput painting. Subjects can be the same, especially Krishna-Radha, but Pahari is usually softer, more mountainous, lyrical, and inward. Britannica's Chamba object entry can serve as a concrete reference for Pahari mythic subject and miniature style. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Varaha-painting) Rajasthani painting tends to have stronger color, flatter structure, and more theatrical energy. If an image mainly has strong red background, hard outline, and courtly festival, it leans Rajasthani; if it has hills, delicate nature, and love emotion, it leans Pahari/Kangra.
