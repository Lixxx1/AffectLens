# French Naturalism

French Naturalism extends from Realism and is especially associated with late nineteenth-century artists such as Jules Bastien-Lepage. The Art Story's Naturalism overview can be used as a reference for observable reality, environment, and everyday experience. [The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/naturalism/) It focuses on bodily condition, social conditions, and natural light in real environments rather than idealized Academic poses. Britannica notes Bastien-Lepage's reputation for outdoor rural scenes and his relation to the rise of Naturalism. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jules-Bastien-Lepage)

When identifying it, ask whether both people and environment are carefully observed. Peasants, fields, grasslands, village roads, labor tools, plain clothing, and tired bodies often appear in natural light. The image may be softer and more outdoor than Courbet-like Realism, but more concerned with figures and social condition than Impressionism. Detail is truthful but not showy; narrative is restrained; figures are ordinary people in their environment.

Emotionally, French Naturalism often has low to medium arousal and complex valence. Natural light and pale air can bring plainness, warmth, or softness; labor fatigue, poverty, and solitary posture lower valence. It does not rely on political slogans. It creates sympathy, silence, and social distance through everyday facts. Encyclopedia.com's realism and naturalism resource can serve as a reference for both movements' concern with daily reality, while Naturalism gives more weight to environment and conditions. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/realism-and-naturalism)
