# Peredvizhniki / Russian Naturalist Realism

Peredvizhniki, or the Wanderers / Itinerants, are an important direction related to Russian Realism and Naturalism in the nineteenth century. Tretyakov Gallery presents them as a core subject in Russian art history, emphasizing traveling exhibitions addressed to the public and images of Russian society, people, landscape, and national life. [Tretyakov Gallery](https://www.tretyakovgallery.ru/exhibitions/o/peredvizhniki-/?lang=en) The movement overlaps with Naturalism because it values truthful observation, social conditions, and lived environment, but it often carries stronger moral, national, and social concern. The Art Story's Peredvizhniki overview can serve as a supplementary reference for this social and national realist context. [The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/peredvizhniki/)

When identifying it, look for Russian villages, peasants, intellectuals, religious or moral scenes, social conflict, national landscape, or solemn portraiture. Figures may carry stronger narrative and moral weight than in French Naturalism, and color may be deeper or more dramatic. Tretyakov Gallery Magazine's article on Repin and his contemporaries can serve as a reference for portraiture, social subject matter, and Russian realist atmosphere. [Tretyakov Gallery Magazine](https://www.tretyakovgallerymagazine.com/articles/4-2005-09/world-ilya-repin-and-his-contemporaries) Landscapes may show broad Russian land, forests, rivers, snow, and sky with both truthfulness and national feeling.

Emotionally, this direction often has complex or negative valence: sympathy, solemnity, melancholy, national feeling, social conscience, and moral pressure may appear. Arousal depends on narrative intensity. Portraits and landscapes may be low to medium; historical or social conflict may be medium-high. If the image emphasizes labor, poverty, or institutional problems, it may lean toward social realism; if it emphasizes environmental truth and humane observation, Naturalism remains relevant.
