# Lyrical Abstraction Direction

## Definition and Context

The lyrical abstraction direction sits near Tachisme but emphasizes flowing color, breathing space, lighter traces, and poetic rhythm. It is less driven by impact than gestural Tachisme and less heavy than Matter Painting. Stains, drips, and colors form a more open emotional field.

It can be understood within the broader expansion of postwar international abstraction.

## Image Identification

Look for transparent or semitransparent layers, soft drips, open space, loose color areas, light curves, and a sense of breathing. The picture may not have a central burst; instead, color seems to drift through space.

If the traces appear spontaneous but rhythmic, abstract but not oppressive, the work may lean lyrical. Color is usually clearer and texture lighter than in Matter Painting.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Valence may be positive, neutral, or gently mixed because flowing color and open space can suggest release, contemplation, poetry, and mild pleasure. Arousal is often low to medium unless color conflict or line density is high.

Use breathing space and color flow as evidence for lower arousal while still noting the spontaneous trace inherited from Tachisme.

## Misclassification Warning

Do not place all soft abstraction in the lyrical direction. There should still be spontaneous trace and non-geometric organization, not only decorative color fields. Compared with Color Field painting, lyrical Tachisme usually retains more gesture, drip, and immediacy.
