# Gestural Tachisme

## Definition and Context

Gestural Tachisme emphasizes improvised action, speed, bodily trace, and non-geometric organization in postwar European abstraction. It is related to American Action Painting, but its context is closer to French and European Art Informel.

Lines, drips, sweeps, fast brushwork, and stains are not decoration. They are evidence that an action has taken place.

## Image Identification

Look for quickly swept lines, splashes, drips, rubbing, stains, and irregular color areas. The composition usually lacks stable geometric structure; the center may be loose and the edges may carry leftover action.

The more immediate the line feels, the stronger the gestural reading. Use direction, weight, apparent speed, and repetition density as formal evidence. If the stroke only imitates movement while the structure remains decorative and controlled, be cautious.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is usually high because of speed, density, directional conflict, and bodily force. Valence depends on color and trace quality. Bright and fluid action can feel releasing and exciting; black, dirty, broken, or crowded marks can feel anxious or oppressive.

Name the traces that show fast action, high force, or reduced control. Those are the core emotional evidence.

## Misclassification Warning

Gestural Tachisme is easily confused with Abstract Expressionism / Action Painting. The difference is not a single brushstroke but historical context, scale, American versus European networks, and material handling. If the surface is heavier, earthier, and wound-like, it may shift toward Matter Painting. If it is softer, more transparent, and poetic, it may shift toward the lyrical direction.
