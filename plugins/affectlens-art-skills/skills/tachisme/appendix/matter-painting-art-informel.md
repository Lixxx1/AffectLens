# Matter Painting / Art Informel Direction

## Definition and Context

The Matter Painting / Art Informel direction emphasizes material thickness, rough surfaces, scraping, mud, sand, plaster, built-up pigment, and wound-like texture. It is connected to Tachisme through stains and improvised action, but it gives greater weight to matter itself: the surface becomes the main site of viewing.

Jean Fautrier and Jean Dubuffet are useful postwar European references for thick surfaces, bodily tactility, and traumatic material presence.

## Image Identification

Look for thick paint, ash, mud, sand grains, scrape marks, cracks, dirty color, clots, and opaque surfaces. Lines may be buried inside material. Colors are often low in brightness: earth tones, gray white, dirty black, or dark red.

This direction is not as light as simple dripping. It feels pressed, scraped, wounded, or covered. If the work makes the viewer sense weight, skin, wall, or injury, it likely belongs here.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Valence is often negative or mixed. Heaviness, dirt, scraping, and wound-like surfaces create associations with pressure, disgust, injury, or trauma, especially in postwar European Art Informel. Arousal is not always high; it may be a low, sustained tension. Dense scraping or strong color conflict raises arousal.

Explain how material affects bodily feeling rather than treating it as generic abstract texture.

## Misclassification Warning

Matter Painting can be confused with Art Brut, Nouveau Realisme, and heavy impasto in Abstract Expressionism. Distinguish it by postwar European Art Informel context and by material surface as the core of non-geometric abstraction. If real discarded objects dominate, the work may move toward Nouveau Realisme. If an outsider symbolic system dominates, it may be Art Brut.
