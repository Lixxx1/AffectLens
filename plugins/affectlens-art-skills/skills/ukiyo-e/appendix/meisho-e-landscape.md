# Famous-Place Landscape / Meisho-e

## Definition And Context

Meisho-e refers to famous-place landscape imagery: travel routes, roads, bridges, rivers, Mount Fuji, temples and shrines, rain and snow, seasons, and well-known sites. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/) In late Edo ukiyo-e, Hokusai and Hiroshige elevated landscape prints, making travel and famous geography part of popular visual consumption. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide) [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39799) These works are landscapes, but they are also travel imagination and local memory within the publishing market. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/)

## Visual Identification

Look for place names, roads, bridges, travelers, post stations, Mount Fuji, waves, rain lines, snow scenes, moonlit nights, and bold compositional cropping; these all point toward famous-place landscape and travel viewing. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide) Hiroshige-like landscapes often include rain, snow, roads, and journey atmosphere; Hokusai-like landscapes may be more structurally organized and visually forceful. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide) [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39799) Colors are flat, outlines are clear, and space often combines bird's-eye view, distance, and daring foregrounds, matching the flattened visual language of ukiyo-e woodblock landscape. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/) If place and season are central, consider meisho-e. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide)

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Meisho-e often has positive, nostalgic, or poetic valence because it turns famous sites, travel experience, and seasonal feeling into collectible visual memory. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/) Snow, moon, and rain scenes can create quietness, travel feeling, and light melancholy; giant waves, strong diagonals, storms, and foreground cropping raise arousal. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide) Fuji, bridges, and roads often bring stable emotion or a sense of extension toward distance. [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39799) Distinguish calm famous-place imagery from high-impact nature imagery: Under the Wave off Kanagawa, for example, raises arousal through the giant wave, boats, and forceful composition. [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39799)

## Common Misreadings

Do not classify every Japanese landscape woodblock as ukiyo-e meisho-e. Look for Edo publishing context, flat color areas, and the consumer culture of famous places. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/) Shin-hanga landscapes are more twentieth-century, softer in light and shadow, and often more refined in printing; ukiyo-e meisho-e is more tied to Edo-period famous sites, travel, and mass publishing. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/hiroshige-artist-open-road/hiroshige-large-print-guide) If the image is a modern tourist pastiche, state that it borrows ukiyo-e language. [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39799)
