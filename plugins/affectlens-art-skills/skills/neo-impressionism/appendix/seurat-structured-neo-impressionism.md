# Seurat-Like Structured Neo-Impressionism

Seurat-like Neo-Impressionism centers on rigorous composition, static figures, clear contours, scientific color, and monument-like modern-life scenes. The Met's Neo-Impressionism materials can serve as a general reference for Seurat, Signac, and systematic color practice. Unlike Impressionism, this direction does not emphasize spontaneous brushwork; it organizes modern suburbs, riverbanks, groups of figures, and light into stable structure. The National Gallery's material on *Bathers at Asnieres* notes Seurat's movement toward pointillism and complementary color juxtaposition.

## Recognition

Look for figures that seem frozen in order. Banks, trees, rivers, clothing, figures, and architecture are calmly arranged. Even when dots are used, composition may feel like classical relief or fresco. Britannica's Pointillism entry can support recognition of small dots and optical mixture. Compared with Signac, Seurat is more restrained, still, and structural; compared with Impressionism, he is less instantaneous and more ordered.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is often low to medium. Stable composition and small dots reduce impulsiveness, while bright light brings calm or clarity. Silent figures, social distance, and mechanical order can make valence feel cool or slightly alienated. In analysis, note the tension between bright light and emotional stillness.
