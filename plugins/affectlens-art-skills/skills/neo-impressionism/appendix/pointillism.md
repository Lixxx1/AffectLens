# Pointillism

Pointillism is the most visually recognizable technique associated with Neo-Impressionism. It uses small round or near-round points of pure color placed next to one another so colors mix optically in the viewer's eye. Britannica links Pointillism to Seurat, Signac, and Neo-Impressionism. The Met also distinguishes Divisionism from Pointillism, noting that precise dot application is one implementation of Neo-Impressionist color separation.

## Recognition

Look for an image made from many regular small dots rather than free short strokes. From far away, forms and light merge; close up, independent red, yellow, blue, green, violet, and other dots are visible. The finer and denser the dots, the calmer and more stable the image; the larger and more contrasting the dots, the higher the arousal. The key is not simply "having dots," but dots serving optical mixture and color theory. Khan Academy's Neo-Impressionist color theory material can support this judgment.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Pointillism often produces clarity, rationality, brightness, stillness, or a faint mechanical quality. Regular dots lower spontaneity and make valence neutral or positive. Strong complementary color and dense vibration raise arousal, creating flicker and visual activity. In analysis, identify dot size, density, contrast, and viewing distance.
