# Nordic / Munch Symbolism

This direction emphasizes anxiety, death, illness, sexuality, jealousy, loneliness, and existential dread. Edvard Munch is the key reference, but the broader Nordic mood may also include twilight landscapes, shoreline spaces, dark interiors, and simplified figures under emotional pressure.

Look for compressed space, staring faces, elongated bodies, strong contours, sickly color, red skies, dark water, and figures isolated from one another. Valence is often negative or deeply mixed. Arousal can be medium to high when the line vibrates, color clashes, or the figure's gaze feels psychologically exposed.

Do not classify every melancholy landscape here. The stronger evidence is psychological intensity, symbolic human presence, and a mood of existential pressure.
