# Visionary / Mediumistic Art

## Definition and Historical Context

Visionary / mediumistic art refers to works driven by intense inner visions, spiritual experience, automatic writing, mystical revelation, or mediumistic ideas of creation. The American Visionary Art Museum defines visionary art as art produced by self-taught makers from an inner personal vision, with the inner drive of the creative act as the key point. [American Visionary Art Museum](https://www.avam.org/visionary-art-definition) Art Brut contexts also include creators such as Augustin Lesage and Fleury Joseph Crépin, who are associated with spiritualist or mediumistic experience. [Collection de l'Art Brut](https://artbrut.ch/en/what-is-raw-art) Aloïse Corbaz is another useful reference for understanding intense inner image systems, figure visions, and the Art Brut context. [Collection de l'Art Brut](https://artbrut.ch/en/artists/aloise)

These works should not be reduced to "religious painting" or "fantasy painting." They often resemble a visual revelation unfolding over time: the image is not made to represent the external world, but to visualize inner order, mystical messages, cosmic structures, or spiritual relations. [National Galleries Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/art-brut)

## Visual Identification

- **Centered or symmetrical structure**: Shrines, faces, spiritual figures, radiating patterns, or architectural hierarchies often unfold from the center.
- **Automatic-writing quality**: Lines, text, symbols, or decoration seem continuously generated, with an uninterrupted rhythm.
- **Spiritual maps**: Celestial bodies, angels, ritual spaces, energy paths, mystical architecture, or cosmic systems recur.
- **Fine decoration**: Small forms, line nets, dots, patterns, and writing create high-density surfaces.
- **Personal grammar of revelation**: The image may not be fully explainable through ordinary religious iconography.

## Emotion and Valence / Arousal

Valence in visionary / mediumistic art often moves among comfort, awe, mystery, and unease. Symmetry and order may increase stability, while bright colors and sacred figures may lean positive; over-dense line networks, endless repetition, and unexplained symbols raise arousal and may create absorption or pressure. Arousal usually depends on density, color intensity, line speed, and whether the visual system gives the viewer room to pause.

## Common Misreadings

- Do not classify every religious subject as visionary art; the key is inner vision, personal system, and self-taught / marginal context.
- Do not force word-like symbols into translation unless the artwork information gives their meaning.
- Do not use "mad" or "grotesque" as primary judgment terms; describe visual structure, rhythm, and emotional evidence.
