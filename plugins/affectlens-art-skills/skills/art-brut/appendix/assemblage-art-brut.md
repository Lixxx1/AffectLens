# Discarded Materials / Assemblage Art Brut

## Definition and Historical Context

Discarded Materials / Assemblage Art Brut refers to images and objects constructed from wood scraps, metal, packaging, string, waste paper, fabric, bottle caps, old toys, tools, armatures, parts, or everyday remnants. It overlaps with modern assemblage and installation art, but in an Art Brut context the material is often not an art-historical strategy. It is matter that the creator has at hand, can transform, and can charge with private meaning. [Collection de l'Art Brut](https://artbrut.ch/en/what-is-raw-art)

Dubuffet himself valued rough materials and non-academic surfaces; National Galleries Scotland's description of Art Brut mentions his interest in untrained creators and coarse materials. [National Galleries Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/art-brut) In appreciation, material source should be treated as emotional evidence: discarded matter can carry associations of life, poverty, memory, humor, talismanic protection, labor, or damage. [Smithsonian American Art Museum](https://americanart.si.edu/art/highlights/folk) At the same time, the work should be placed back into folk / self-taught / outsider art institutional contexts rather than interpreted only through contemporary installation material theory. [American Folk Art Museum](https://folkartmuseum.org/about/)

## Visual Identification

- **Recognizable old objects**: Packaging, sheet metal, wood, rope, bottle caps, fabric, or tools still retain traces of their former use.
- **Handmade joining**: Nails, binding, stitching, glue, wrapping, and irregular assembly reveal the making process.
- **Rough surface**: Damage, rust, stains, fading, ragged edges, and nonindustrial alignment are common.
- **Talisman / doll / architectural quality**: Assemblages may resemble small idols, masks, animals, tools, houses, or private altars.
- **Material density**: The denser the material, the more likely arousal rises; sparse arrangements may feel more memorial or still-life-like.

## Emotion and Valence / Arousal

Discarded-material assemblage often creates roughness, humor, absurdity, unease, intimacy, or resistance. Valence depends strongly on the material condition: bright old toys and everyday objects can feel warm or comic; rusty iron, torn cloth, sharp edges, and damaged bodies lower valence. Arousal is usually determined by material conflict, spatial density, and object sharpness. If materials are piled like a talisman or barrier, the emotion may be both protective and oppressive.

In output, you can write: "The old wood and metal wire retain traces of discard, lowering valence and creating a damaged feeling; the handmade binding and doll-like form make the work feel talismanic, adding mystery and defense; the material density makes arousal medium-high."

## Common Misreadings

- Do not classify every old-object collage as Art Brut; modern art, Arte Povera, ecological art, and folk craft also use discarded materials.
- Combine creator context, private system, and distance from academic / market norms.
- If the work is highly conceptual, exhibition-oriented, or industrially well fabricated, it may be closer to contemporary installation or Nouveau Réalisme.
