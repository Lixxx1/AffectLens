# Chinese Cultural Revolution Propaganda

This direction emphasizes revolutionary youth, workers, peasants, soldiers, Mao imagery, red flags, radiant light, collective bodies, model operas, and militant optimism. Figures are often idealized, healthy, disciplined, and oriented toward a revolutionary future.

Valence is designed to be strongly positive within the propaganda frame: loyalty, fervor, purity, and collective confidence. Arousal is high because of red color, upward gestures, sharp contrast, crowds, and emphatic slogans or symbols. From a critical perspective, the same cues can feel coercive, absolutist, or emotionally overwhelming.

Distinguish it from broader Chinese modern art by its explicit revolutionary iconography, slogan-like clarity, and didactic political function.
