# Italian Transavanguardia

Italian Transavanguardia is the Italian version of the broader Neo-Expressionist return to painting. It was named by Achille Bonito Oliva in the late 1970s and is associated with Sandro Chia, Francesco Clemente, Enzo Cucchi, Mimmo Paladino, and Nicola De Maria. Tate describes it as part of the international revival of expressive painting in the late 1970s and 1980s. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/t/transavanguardia) Castello di Rivoli also stresses its renewed entry into painting through history, poetry, and fragments of image. [Castello di Rivoli](https://www.castellodirivoli.org/en/mostra/transavanguardia/)

To identify it, look for myth, classicism, bodies, masks, animals, text, symbols, and historical collage. Transavanguardia is not only rough emotion; it emphasizes crossing history, stylistic wandering, and personal allegory. Treccani's entry can serve as a reference for the naming, Italian artists, and postmodern stylistic wandering. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/transavanguardia/) Chia often uses heroic or mock-heroic bodies; Clemente leans toward body, identity, and Indian experience; Cucchi is darker and more catastrophic; Paladino emphasizes symbols, masks, and archaic feeling; De Maria is more chromatic and lyrical.

Emotionally, Transavanguardia often has mixed valence: classical nostalgia, absurdity, mystery, corporeality, satire, and crisis coexist. Arousal is usually medium-high, depending on symbol density and brush intensity. Compared with German Neue Wilde it may feel more poetic, mythic, and playful; compared with American Neo-Expressionism, it usually has less consumer and street text.
