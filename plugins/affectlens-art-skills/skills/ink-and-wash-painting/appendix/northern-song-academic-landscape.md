# Northern Song / Academic Landscape

Northern Song and academic landscape emphasizes structure, layered composition, mountain mass, texture strokes, mist, near-far relationships, and monumental space. National Palace Museum resources can serve as references for landscape, inscriptions, scroll formats, and traditional types. These works often build a large cosmic feeling through towering mountains, deep valleys, streams, bridges, pavilions, travelers, trees, and rocks, making human figures appear small. Compared with literati ink's pale personal mindscape, Northern Song/academic landscape stresses landscape order, spatial construction, and nature's sublime force.

## Recognition

First check whether the mountain has a clear structural skeleton. Layered texture strokes and tonal ink build rock, ridges, woods, and clouds. Paths, streams, bridges, houses, and figures guide the eye into distance. Britannica's Chinese painting resource can serve as a reference for scroll space, brush-ink, line, and viewing mode. The image often contains foreground trees/rocks, middle-ground mountains, and distant clouded peaks. Space is not Western single-point perspective, but it remains inhabitable and traversable.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

This mode often evokes sublimity, solemnity, calm, reverence, or remote clarity. Huge mountains and deep space raise psychological scale and produce medium-to-high arousal, while stable composition and pale mist keep emotion calm. Valence is often neutral-to-positive awe, but solitary pavilions, winter scenes, cold forests, or travelers can add loneliness or nostalgia. Judge by mountain pressure, misty empty space, and the smallness of human figures.

## Common Confusion

This direction can be confused with blue-green landscape and Western landscape. If mineral blue-green color dominates, it may overlap with blue-green landscape. The Cleveland Museum of Art's *Palace Landscape* can be used as a courtly architectural/blue-green adjacent reference. Chinese ink landscape emphasizes ink, texture strokes, and shifting viewpoint; Western landscape more often emphasizes fixed light source and optical perspective.
