# Bird, Flower, Bamboo, and Rock

The bird, flower, bamboo, and rock direction uses subjects such as plum, orchid, bamboo, chrysanthemum, pine, stone, birds, flowers, narcissus, and lingzhi to carry moral character, season, wishes, and literati symbolism. It can be meticulous or freehand, but in ink-and-wash contexts it often emphasizes ink line, branch-leaf rhythm, empty space, inscriptions, and the moral suggestion of plants and animals. National Palace Museum / Google Arts & Culture material on Yun Shouping's *The Five Purities* explains how pine, bamboo, water, moon, and plum can symbolize a gentleman's pure character.

## Recognition

Look for whether objects are transformed into ink-line character. Bamboo leaves are made through individual strokes; rocks gain form through texture strokes and pale ink; plum branches use angular branch lines and dotted blossoms; birds and flowers may use concise line-ink with light color. Wen Tong's ink bamboo tradition is a reference for how line, leaf movement, and literati virtue establish the image. Simple subjects can carry complex emotion because every stroke's speed, direction, and tonal density matters. Bamboo may imply humility and uprightness; plum suggests life in coldness; rock suggests firmness, solitude, or stability.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is usually low to medium. Sparse bamboo, pale ink, empty space, and solitary rocks create elegance, independence, quiet observation, and literati atmosphere. Dense flowers and birds, strong ink, or active bird movement raise arousal. Valence depends on symbolic combinations: plum and bamboo may feel clear and upright; flower-bird subjects may feel lively or joyful; a lone bird on a cold branch may feel lonely. Do not automatically read flower-bird imagery as happy; position, season, and ink tone can alter emotion.

## Common Confusion

This direction can be confused with gongbi bird-and-flower painting and decorative botanical painting. Gongbi bird-and-flower emphasizes fine color, feather and leaf detail, and layered washes; ink bird-flower-bamboo-rock emphasizes brush-ink, empty space, and symbolic character. Decorative botanical painting may be attractive but lacks calligraphic line, inscriptions/seals, and literati symbolism. In analysis, explain how brush and ink create character rather than merely listing plant names.
