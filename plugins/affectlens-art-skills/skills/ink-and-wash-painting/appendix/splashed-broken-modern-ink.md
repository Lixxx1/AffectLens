# Splashed Ink / Broken Ink / Modern Ink

Splashed ink, broken ink, and modern ink emphasize the relation among ink, water, paper, speed, and chance. Splashed ink lets large ink masses spread, flow, and overlap naturally. Broken ink adds different ink densities onto wet or dry layers, creating rupture, penetration, and depth. Modern ink further absorbs abstraction, photography, installation, concept art, and international modern art, turning traditional brush-and-ink into contemporary visual language. Norton Museum material on Zhang Daqian notes his late expressive splashed-ink and splashed-color landscapes, which expanded the possibilities of ink.

## Recognition

Look for ink traces that cannot be fully controlled: spreading, flow marks, pouring, water collisions, layered staining, or abstract planes. Traditional landscape traces may remain as mountains, clouds, water, or trees, but they are reconstructed by ink masses, color fields, and water marks. Modern ink may also use large scale, extreme empty space, repeated brushwork, abstract composition, or calligraphic action. Paper absorption, feathered edges, and tonal layering are central evidence.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal can range from low to high. Large pale ink areas and empty space can feel contemplative, airy, and floating. Dense ink explosions, poured traces, and strong black-white contrast create high-arousal energy, storm, collapse, or excitement. Valence depends on ink weight and pictorial breathing: light ink can feel distant and clear; heavy ink can feel oppressive; flowing traces can feel free or uneasy. Compared with traditional literati ink, modern ink more often makes material itself the emotional protagonist.

## Common Confusion

This direction can be confused with Abstract Expressionism or Western watercolor. Abstract Expressionism also uses pouring and action, but the material, paper-ink behavior, empty space, calligraphic quality, and Chinese ink history differ. Western watercolor may also bloom and stain, but it usually depends more on transparent color and optical scene. If the work is fully abstract, explain that the classification evidence comes from material and brush-ink handling rather than subject.
