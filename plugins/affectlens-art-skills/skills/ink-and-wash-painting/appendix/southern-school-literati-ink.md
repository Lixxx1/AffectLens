# Southern School / Literati Ink

Southern School or literati ink usually refers to Chinese ink painting centered on scholar cultivation, calligraphic brushwork, pale ink, empty space, inscriptions, seals, and personal mindscape. It does not aim at polished representation. It emphasizes personality, breath, and viewing rhythm within brush and ink. Met and Britannica resources both emphasize that poetry, calligraphy, painting, and seals often form a complete work, with brush-ink carrying cultural cultivation and emotion.

## Recognition

Look for whether the image is sparse yet resonant. Mountains, rocks, trees, thatched huts, distant hills, bamboo, or stones may be completed with brief brushwork and pale ink. Large empty space can become cloud, water, sky, or mental space. Inscriptions and seals are not secondary decoration; they affect rhythm and cultural context. Lines may be dry, wet, dark, pale, rough, and moist together. Compared with academic landscape, literati ink does not seek monumental structure or meticulous form as much as brush-ink flavor and personal feeling.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is usually low to medium. Pale ink, empty space, solitary pavilions, distant mountains, and sparse trees create distance, solitude, leisure, cool quiet, reclusion, or contemplation. Valence is not always positive: emptiness and pale ink can be peaceful, but also express disappointment, separation, or faint melancholy. Sudden dense ink, urgent dry brush, or crowded inscriptions can raise arousal and reveal inner turbulence.

## Common Confusion

Literati ink can be confused with Zen painting or modern abstract ink. Zen painting tends to emphasize abruptness, minimal brushwork, and religious context. Modern abstract ink may turn ink traces into material experiment. Literati ink depends on the combination of poetry-calligraphy-painting-seal, cultivated brushwork, empty space, and personal mindscape. If an image is only a black-and-white landscape but lacks brush rhythm or inscription context, classify cautiously.
