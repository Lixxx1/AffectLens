# Yakusha-e

## Definition And Context

Yakusha-e are kabuki actor images, already important in ukiyo-e. Shin-hanga continues this subject but usually treats actors with more refined woodblock craft, modern portrait awareness, and stage lighting. Art & History Museum's shin-hanga resource can be used as a reference for how shin-hanga renewed traditional subjects through twentieth-century publishing and production. [Art & History Museum](https://www.artandhistory.museum/en/shin-hanga) These works focus on facial makeup, gaze, hand gesture, costume, stage role, and mie poses. Emotional intensity is usually higher than in shin-hanga landscapes or bijin-ga.

## Visual Identification

Look for kabuki makeup, exaggerated eyes, role costume, hand gestures, frontal or bust-length composition, strong facial outline, and stage presence. Shin-hanga yakusha-e may keep ukiyo-e-like forceful contour, but printing is more delicate, and shadow or paper quality may feel more modern. Honolulu Museum of Art's Japanese woodblock print resource can be used for block line, color registration, and paper craft. [Honolulu Museum of Art](https://honolulumuseum.org/japanese-woodblock-prints-q2ct) Expression, eye line, mouth shape, sleeve, and textile pattern are key evidence.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Yakusha-e can reach medium-high arousal because stage expression, gaze, makeup contrast, and role action create tension, solemnity, or theatrical force. Valence depends on the role: heroic poses may lean positive, while tragic, vengeful, or villain roles may be negative or mixed. Unlike shin-hanga landscapes, emotion comes from performance and identity transformation rather than weather and empty space.

## Common Misreadings

Yakusha-e can be mistaken for Edo ukiyo-e. Shin-hanga versions require twentieth-century production context, publisher information, and refined modern print quality. The National Diet Library resource on sosaku-hanga and shin-hanga can help distinguish collaborative shin-hanga production from individual creative-print logic. [National Diet Library](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/imagebank/column/shinhanga) If the image is flat, heavy-outlined, and clearly Edo-period, it may be traditional ukiyo-e; if it is strongly self-carved and individual, it may approach sosaku-hanga.
