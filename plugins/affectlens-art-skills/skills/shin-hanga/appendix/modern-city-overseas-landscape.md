# Modern City / Overseas Landscape

## Definition And Context

Shin-hanga does not only look back to Edo tradition; it also represents twentieth-century modern Japan, international travel, and overseas views. Worcester Art Museum's "Reflections of a Changing Japan" can serve as a reference for shin-hanga within modernizing Japan, urban change, and the movement's evolution. [Worcester Art Museum](https://www.worcesterart.org/exhibitions/reflections-of-a-changing-japan-the-evolution-of-shin-hanga/) Electric lights, Western-style buildings, railways, bridges, harbors, city nights, foreign landmarks, and travel viewpoints may appear. This direction shows shin-hanga's dual nature: traditional woodblock craft combined with modern city, overseas market, travel experience, and Western perspective.

## Visual Identification

Look for electric-light reflections, street perspective, harbors, bridges, modern buildings, railways, foreign temples or European / Asian travel sites. Art & History Museum's shin-hanga resource can be used for traditional woodblock division of labor, modern visual renewal, and continued landscape subjects. [Art & History Museum](https://www.artandhistory.museum/en/shin-hanga) The image still uses carved line, color registration, and gradients, but space is deeper and lighting may approach modern photography or Western painting. In city nights, warm windows, street lamps, and wet roads often create local highlights.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

Valence is often mixed. Modern lights, travel, and novelty create excitement or positive curiosity; empty streets, rainy nights, harbors, and foreign distance bring solitude, nostalgia, or estrangement. Arousal is usually higher than traditional poetic landscape and lower than actor prints. Strong perspective, electric-light contrast, transport, and crowds raise visual activity. Explain how modern elements modify shin-hanga's usual low-arousal quiet.

## Common Misreadings

Modern city / overseas landscape can be mistaken for ordinary tourist prints. Distinguish by block line, printing quality, publication information, paper, and shin-hanga craft. Honolulu Museum of Art's Japanese woodblock print resource can serve as a reference for block line, color registration, and paper craft. [Honolulu Museum of Art](https://honolulumuseum.org/japanese-woodblock-prints-q2ct) Do not exclude shin-hanga only because it uses Western perspective; that is often part of the movement. If the image is photography or digital illustration imitating woodblock style, say it is influenced by shin-hanga rather than classifying it as shin-hanga.
