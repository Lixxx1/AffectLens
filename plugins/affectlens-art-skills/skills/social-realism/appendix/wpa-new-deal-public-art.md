# WPA / New Deal Public Art

## Definition And Context

WPA / New Deal public art refers to the context of United States government art projects and public-building art during the Great Depression. It is not a single style. The National Gallery of Art's "Great Depression" educational resource can be used as a reference for Depression-era public art and American social themes. [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/educational-resources/uncovering-america/great-depression) WPA / New Deal art may include Social Realism, Regionalism, modernism, and educational mural practice. Its core context is government funding, public space, artist employment, community education, and American subject matter. Britannica's Works Progress Administration entry is a useful institutional reference for relief, public works, and employment programs. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Works-Progress-Administration)

## Visual Identification

Check whether the work is connected to post offices, schools, courthouses, libraries, government buildings, public murals, or printmaking programs. Britannica's WPA Federal Art Project entry can serve as a specific reference for federal art commissions, artist employment, and public art. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/WPA-Federal-Art-Project) Common subjects include workers, farmers, construction, local history, transportation, industry, community activity, and cultural diversity. The style may be milder than Social Realism, though it can also be critical. Compositions are usually clear, narrative, and readable for public audiences. If the source is the Federal Art Project, PWAP, Treasury Section, or WPA, include that institutional context in the judgment.

## Emotion: Valence / Arousal

WPA public art often has more mixed valence than Social Realism: it may show hardship, but it also often emphasizes construction, education, public hope, and communal resilience. Arousal is often moderate, depending on mural scale, figure movement, and industrial scenes. If the image is a local-history or labor mural, the emotion may sit between Regionalism's stability and Social Realism's critique. Do not equate WPA with one single emotional tone.

## Common Misreadings

WPA is a project or funding context, not a style label. Do not automatically call every WPA work Social Realism, and do not exclude Regionalism simply because a work is a public mural. Britannica's Social Realism entry can help distinguish the critical social-protest boundary from the broader WPA project context. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Social-Realism-painting) Judge whether the subject criticizes social injustice, emphasizes local identity, serves official public education, and how the work organizes viewer emotion.
