# Social Realism

Social Realism uses representational art to address labor, poverty, inequality, protest, migration, industrial life, and social injustice. It is often urban, political, and reform-minded.

Valence is frequently negative or mixed because hardship and injustice are central, but dignity, solidarity, and resistance can add positive force. Arousal rises with crowding, conflict, gesture, and dramatic light.

Distinguish Social Realism from Socialist Realism: Social Realism may criticize existing conditions, while Socialist Realism often idealizes state socialist goals and heroic labor.
