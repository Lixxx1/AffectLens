# Photorealism / Hyperrealism

Photorealism and Hyperrealism use extreme precision, photographic reference, reflective surfaces, sharp detail, and enlarged everyday subjects. They are realist in appearance, but their emotional effect often comes from mediation, scrutiny, scale, and uncanny exactness.

Valence is often neutral or cool, but can become uneasy when detail feels too intense, sterile, or artificial. Arousal depends on scale, contrast, and surface complexity.

Do not confuse technical exactitude with nineteenth-century Realism. Ask whether the image is studying contemporary visual culture, photography, consumer objects, bodies, or perception itself.
