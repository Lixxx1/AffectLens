# Postwar International Direction

## Definition and Context

Postwar creative prints expanded through international exhibitions, modern design, abstraction, and folk-art contexts. Artists absorbed abstraction, construction, Buddhist and folk imagery, modern graphic design, and global print exchange. Sosaku Hanga became part of international modern printmaking rather than only a domestic Japanese revival.

Onchi's modern design, abstraction, and print experiments can be understood as precursors to this postwar expansion.

## Image Identification

Look for freer abstract composition, strong color blocks, visible wood grain, rubbing, heavy ink, contrasting fine and rough cuts, and modern design rhythm. Subjects may come from Buddhism, folk culture, city life, nature, or pure form, but the work should still show the creative-print ideal of personal carving and printing.

Compared with early directions, postwar works may be bolder in large black-white areas, intense pattern, or nonfigurative structure, and they more readily enter American collections, museum exhibitions, and international modern-art narratives.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal ranges widely. Minimal color blocks and wood grain can create calm or contemplation; intense black-white, rough line, religious imagery, and high contrast raise arousal. Valence ranges from quiet to fervent, because postwar creative prints can be coolly abstract or intensely alive through carving, folk, and religious imagery.

Use postwar modernity as background, but ground the emotional reading in line density, color strength, knife marks, and image theme.

## Misclassification Warning

This direction can be confused with European or American abstract printmaking because it participates in international modernism. Check for self-drawn / self-carved / self-printed practice, woodblock technique, Japanese paper or printing traces, and the artist's Sosaku Hanga context. Do not exclude it merely because it is abstract, and do not call it traditional ukiyo-e just because it uses Japanese signs.
