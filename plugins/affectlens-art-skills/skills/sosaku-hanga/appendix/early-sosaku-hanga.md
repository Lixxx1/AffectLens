# Early Sosaku Hanga

## Definition and Context

Early Sosaku Hanga centers on the move away from the collaborative publication systems of ukiyo-e and shin-hanga. It emphasizes that the artist personally conceives, carves, and prints the work. Printmaking shifts from reproduction or publishing craft toward individual original expression.

Early works often include black-white woodcuts, rural subjects, figures, landscapes, rough carved lines, and visible handmade irregularity.

## Image Identification

Look for obvious knife marks, forceful rough lines, simplified color, visible wood grain, and pressure marks. These are not necessarily signs of poor craft; they may intentionally emphasize the artist's hand.

The subject may still be Japanese figures or landscapes, but the treatment is more personal and less polished than publisher-led shin-hanga.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is often higher than in shin-hanga because the cuts, rough lines, black-white contrast, and personal touch feel more direct. Valence depends on subject and carving method. Rural or everyday subjects can feel plain and positive; hard black-white or isolated figures can feel heavy or tense.

Use handmade trace as emotional evidence, not subject alone.

## Misclassification Warning

Do not mistake early creative prints for low-quality ukiyo-e or tourist woodblocks. The key is the self-drawn, self-carved, self-printed ideal, personal expression, and modernist context. Compared with German Expressionist woodcut, Japanese creative prints may be rough but do not necessarily share the same urban anxiety or social critique.
