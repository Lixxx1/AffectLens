# Onchi Koshiro Direction

## Definition and Context

Onchi Koshiro was a key figure in the Sosaku Hanga movement. He was not only a printmaker but also a poet, editor, designer, and organizer. His direction often combines poetry, abstraction, photographic sensibility, book design, and woodblock experiment.

Onchi's work shows that creative prints need not remain within traditional Japanese subject matter. They can enter modernist psychology, musical rhythm, and abstract structure.

## Image Identification

Look for simplified geometry, abstract color blocks, photography-like cropping, poetic titles, book-design sensibility, and controlled hand printing. Knife marks are not always rough or aggressive; they may be restrained, precise, and meditative.

If figures or landscapes appear, they are often transformed into emotional structures rather than traditional narrative scenes. A modern graphic design feeling combined with woodblock handmade evidence can suggest this direction.

## Emotion and Valence/Arousal

Arousal is often low to medium unless color blocks or lines create strong conflict. The modernist language creates inward tension through rhythm, cropping, and abstract relationships. Valence is often neutral, poetic, introspective, or gently melancholic.

Do not force a concrete story. Explain how form carries psychological or poetic feeling.

## Misclassification Warning

This direction can be mistaken for general modern abstract printmaking. Distinguish it by the Sosaku Hanga self-carved / self-printed concept, woodblock material traces, and Onchi's Japanese modern printmaking context. Compared with shin-hanga, it lacks publisher-centered polish and emphasizes unified personal conception and making.
