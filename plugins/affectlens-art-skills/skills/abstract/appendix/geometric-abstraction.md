# Geometric Abstraction

Geometric Abstraction is non-representational art that builds the image from geometric shapes, lines, grids, and flat pictorial space. The Met traces its development from Cubism's challenge to traditional perspective and imitation, through flattened structure, linear grids, and abstract space. Analytic Cubism foregrounded planar fragmentation, while Synthetic Cubism introduced flatter shapes and abstract arrangements that helped form the basis of later geometric abstraction.

## Key Features

- **Geometry and grids:** squares, rectangles, circles, straight lines, verticals, horizontals, and repeated grid structures.
- **Two-dimensional space:** the image emphasizes flatness and often rejects traditional perspective.
- **Limited palette:** solid colors, primary colors, black, white, and sharply separated color blocks are common.
- **Order and rationality:** symmetry, proportion, sequence, and grids create stability, balance, and a low-arousal feeling.
- **Historical context:** Piet Mondrian's Neo-Plasticism used black straight lines and primary color rectangles to pursue an abstract universal order; Kazimir Malevich's Suprematism used floating geometric forms to pursue a spiritual absolute.

## Emotion and Appreciation Tips

Geometric Abstraction usually evokes rationality, clarity, calm, balance, or impersonal order. When analyzing emotion, focus on shape distribution, grid rhythm, color contrast, symmetry, and how the viewer's eye moves through the composition. Stable grids tend toward lower arousal; sharp diagonals, dense repetition, or high-contrast colors can raise arousal.
