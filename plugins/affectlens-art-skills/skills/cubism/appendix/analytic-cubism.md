# Analytic Cubism

Analytic Cubism usually refers to the phase around 1908-1912 in which Picasso and Braque decomposed objects into dense planes, shallow space, and multiple viewpoints. The Metropolitan Museum of Art's essay on Cubism is a useful reference for Picasso, Braque, and the context of multi-viewpoint decomposition. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/cube/hd_cube.htm) Its purpose is not to decorate objects with geometric patterns, but to "analyze" the structure of a violin, glass, portrait, tabletop, or interior space on the canvas. MoMA and Guggenheim Bilbao both emphasize that Analytic Cubism is relatively restrained in color, often using gray, brown, ocher, dark green, and black, reducing color narrative so that the viewer attends to structure, angle, and shifts in viewpoint. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/cubism/analytic-and-synthetic-cubism) [Guggenheim Museum Bilbao](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibition/cubismo-analitico)

For identification, first ask whether the object is still present but difficult to recognize at a glance. The image may contain clues such as strings, cup rims, nose bridges, table edges, or newspaper letters, but these clues are cut into many triangles, trapezoids, curved planes, and short broken lines. Traditional foreground / background depth is compressed, and objects seem unfolded into a shallow relief-like surface. MoMA's distinction between Analytic and Synthetic Cubism can support this reading. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/cubism/analytic-and-synthetic-cubism) If one portrait contains frontal eyes, a profile nose, and contours from different angles at once, or if a tabletop appears both frontal and seen from above, the painting is likely working through multiple viewpoints.

Emotionally, Analytic Cubism often creates intellect, coolness, seriousness, distance, and slight unease. Low-saturation color lowers emotional warmth and makes the image feel neutral or cool. Dense fragments, compressed space, and unstable boundaries raise arousal. If the fragments are very fine and the object is hard to recognize, the viewer must actively assemble the image, producing cognitive pressure and higher arousal. If the plane organization is stable and the tonal range is soft, the result may be a lower-arousal rational contemplation.

Common confusion points include decorative geometry and Constructivism. Decorative geometry may only have colorful polygonal surfaces without conflict among viewpoints. Constructivism leans more toward engineering, design, propaganda, or spatial structure. The key evidence for Analytic Cubism is that an object is decomposed from multiple angles, space is flattened, and color serves structure rather than pure geometric beauty. Do not simply say "it looks like broken glass"; specify which object clues are decomposed and how that decomposition changes the emotional viewing experience.
